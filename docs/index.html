    Why the in-range companies are cheap

    For every company in or below its range, Claude read company reports and major news outlets to judge whether the fall looks deserved. Open one for the sources.

    These companies are not researched. The page screens them with generic tests: a price at or below 20 times owner cash, debt under 3 years of cash, and sales not shrinking. One that shows up here is a lead to look into, and Claude can add it to the watchlist with tests that fit it.

    How to read it. Color is the signal. Green means the price is inside the company's fat-pitch range and the business passes its tests. Pale green is within 20% of the range. Amber is in range with an unclear business, red is in range with a broken one, and grey is still above its range.

    Where the numbers come from. Prices and quarterly financials come from Yahoo Finance. Prices refresh about every half hour on US market days and financials once a day. Ranges, test thresholds and return assumptions are my judgment and are not backtested. A few figures, such as net retention, are typed in from company reports and carry their date. General research, not personal investment advice.
